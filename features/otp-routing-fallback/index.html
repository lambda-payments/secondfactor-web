Automatic OTP Routing and Fallback

SecondFactor sends each code over the lowest-cost channel that can reach the number, and switches to the next channel when delivery isn’t confirmed within eight seconds.

What Is Automatic OTP Routing and Fallback?

OTP routing is the decision about which channel carries a verification code: SMS, WhatsApp, Viber or RCS. Fallback is what happens when that channel doesn’t deliver. The code is sent again over a different channel, so the user still receives it.

With SecondFactor, both happen inside one API request. You send a phone number. The routing engine picks the lowest-cost channel your account has enabled that can reach it, waits eight seconds for a delivery receipt, and moves to the next channel if none arrives.

Your application doesn’t hold a channel order, a retry timer or a second provider integration.

  2. +8.0sNo delivery receipt. Switching channel
  3. Attempt 2SMS — delivered
  4. Verify200 OK · code matched

Lower Delivery Costs Without Losing Deliveries

Routing on price cuts what you pay for each verification. Fallback keeps the cheaper route from costing you signups when it fails.

Lowest-Cost Route First

Each request is priced for its destination, and the cheapest channel that can reach the number is tried first.

Fallback in Eight Seconds

When the first channel doesn’t confirm delivery, the next eligible channel takes over without a second request from your app.

No Routing Code to Maintain

The send request has no channel parameter. Channel order, timers and retries stay out of your codebase.

Every Route on Record

The verification shows the channel that delivered, the delivery status and what the send cost.

How Routing and Fallback Work

Four things happen between your send request and the code reaching your user.

You Send a Phone Number

Post the number in E.164 format to the Verifications endpoint. SecondFactor generates the code and returns a verification SID.

Eligible Channels Are Found

The engine keeps the channels that are enabled on your account, can reach this number and are allowed by your delivery rules.

The Cheapest Channel Sends

The code goes out over the lowest-cost channel left. A delivery receipt within eight seconds ends the routing.

The Next Channel Takes Over

With no receipt after eight seconds, the next eligible channel is tried, until one delivers or every channel has failed.

One Request, No Channel Logic

The send request carries the destination and nothing about delivery. The 201 response means the send was accepted, not that the message has arrived.

To follow delivery, fetch the verification or listen for webhooks. delivery_status moves from PENDING to DELIVERED or FAILED, and channel holds the channel that delivered the code.

What Decides the Route

A channel is only tried when it is eligible for that request. Four things decide eligibility and order.

Channels Enabled on Your Account

Only channels you have connected are considered, with their sender and template setup complete. Compare the channels.

What the Recipient Can Receive

A channel has to be able to reach the number. Channels that can’t reach it are left out of the route.

The Price for the Destination

Eligible channels are ordered by cost for the destination, lowest first. See pricing by country.

Your Delivery Rules

Destinations blocked by your delivery rules are refused before anything is sent, and refused sends are not charged.

Follow Every Delivery

Routing decisions are visible after the fact, so a missing code can be traced to a channel and a status.

Delivery Webhooks

Receive a signed otp.delivered event when a carrier confirms delivery and otp.failed when every channel has failed. Webhook reference.

Verification Status

Fetch a verification at any time to read its delivery status, delivering channel and price. Fetching is free.

OTP Logs

Review the destination, channel, status and cost of each request in your dashboard.

Frequently Asked Questions

Write to support@secondfactor.ai for anything not covered here.

Can I Choose Which Channel an OTP Uses?+

The send request has no channel parameter. SecondFactor chooses from the channels enabled on your account, so the way to keep a channel out of routing is to leave it disabled.

How Long Does SecondFactor Wait Before Switching Channels?+

Eight seconds. If the first channel has not confirmed delivery by then, the next eligible channel is tried.

Do I Need Every Channel Connected for Fallback to Work?+

No. Fallback needs at least two enabled channels that can reach the recipient. With one channel connected, requests are sent on that channel only.

Am I Charged Again When an OTP Falls Back to Another Channel?+

Each accepted send is a single charge. Sending a new code to the same number is a new send and is charged again, so limit how often users can press “send again”.

What Happens When Every Channel Fails?+

The verification’s delivery status becomes FAILED and you receive an otp.failed webhook. A number that every channel rejects is refused for up to 30 days, and refused sends are not charged.

How Do I See Which Channel Delivered a Code?+

Read the channel field on the verification, listen for the otp.delivered webhook, or open the OTP logs in your dashboard.

Can I Test Routing and Fallback Before Integrating?+

Yes. Use your free credits in the PIE Playground to send a real OTP to your own number, inspect the route it took and verify the code you receive.
