Twilio Verify packages code generation, delivery and checking behind a simple API, which is why so many teams start there. As volumes grow or new markets come online, the questions change: what does each verification really cost, which channels can you use, and how much say do you have in routing?

This guide covers alternatives that teams commonly evaluate, the criteria that matter in a migration, and how to decide. Provider details were last checked on the date above.

How we evaluated

We compared each provider against the criteria below, using publicly available documentation and pricing.

  • Cost at your volumes. Total cost per completed verification in your main destination countries, including any per-verification fee and fallback attempts.

  • Migration effort. How closely the API maps to the send and check pattern you already use.

How to choose

The best option depends on your situation more than on any ranking. Start from the constraint that matters most to you.

If cost is the reason you are leaving

Model your last month of traffic against each provider using your real country mix. Savings usually come from a small number of high-volume destinations.

If you need more channels

Shortlist providers that offer WhatsApp, Viber or RCS alongside SMS through one integration, so adding a channel does not mean another vendor.

If you want a low-effort migration

Prefer an API shaped like the one you already call. SecondFactor’s send, check and fetch endpoints are deliberately Twilio-shaped, and the documentation calls out the behaviour differences.

Frequently asked questions

Why do teams look for a Twilio Verify alternative?

The most common reasons are cost at higher volumes, the need for additional delivery channels, and wanting more visibility into or control over how each code is routed.

How hard is it to migrate OTP verification to another provider?

It depends on how closely the new API matches your current send and check calls, and on any sender or template approvals the new channels require. Most teams run both providers in parallel on a slice of traffic before switching fully.

Can I keep SMS and add WhatsApp or other channels?

Yes. Multi-channel providers let you keep SMS and enable other channels over time, typically after completing the sender and template setup each channel requires.
